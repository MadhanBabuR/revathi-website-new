Training & Offerings

Four ways onto the floor.

Every program teaches the same traditional curriculum: vadivus, chuvadus, breath, and in time, weapons. What changes is the format, the pace and who you train beside.

Offline Training

In the kalari · Kozhikode

Regular classes focusing on foundational body mechanics, animal postures, flexibility, and traditional weaponry, taught on the kalari thara itself.

  • Foundational vadivus and chuvadus, built by repetition.
  • Flexibility and conditioning drawn from the art, not added to it.
  • Progression into wooden and then metal weapon forms.
  • Trained barefoot, in person, alongside other students.

Online Training

Live, worldwide

Accessible guidance for practitioners worldwide, bringing authentic Kalari conditioning straight to your space.

  • Live sessions, so posture is corrected as you move.
  • Needs only a camera, clothes you can move in and bare feet.
  • Roughly the space of a yoga mat is enough to begin.
  • Suitable for complete beginners with no martial arts background.

Mindful Movement & Kids Workshops

Short focused sessions

Special sessions designed to improve posture, body alignment, focus, and playful discipline for the younger generation.

  • Posture and alignment work for adults who sit all day.
  • Breath and attention training, taught as skills rather than moods.
  • Kids' camps built around play, coordination and discipline.
  • Run as standalone workshops; dates shared on enquiry.

Personal Training

One to one

Custom one-on-one sessions tailored to individual fitness and martial goals, with flexible scheduling and a customised curriculum.

  • Curriculum shaped around your starting point and your goal.
  • Available online or in the kalari.
  • Flexible scheduling, arranged directly.
  • Useful for performers, athletes and returning practitioners.

Inside a session

The order never changes.

A class opens with the body. Stances and steps first, breath tied to the movement from the very first lesson, conditioning arriving through the sequences rather than bolted on beside them.

Weapons are not a reward for attendance. They are taken up when the foundation is honest enough to hold them, which is a judgement the floor makes, not the calendar.

Starting from nothing

You do not need to arrive ready.

Most people who step onto the thara for the first time have never done a martial art. Kalaripayattu was shaped by generations of bodies, all different, and the beginner curriculum assumes nothing at all.
