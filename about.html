The ancestral roots

Hindustan Kalari Sangham.

At the heart of Kalariwoman lies a deep connection to the rich tradition of Hindustan Kalari Sangham, based in Kozhikode, Kerala. Established in 1950 by Veerasree Sami Gurukkal, the institution has stood for over half a century as a premier sanctuary for the Northern style of Kalaripayattu.

Growing up immersed in this legacy, the practice of Kalari was never merely an extracurricular activity. It was a way of life, a language of survival, grace, and spiritual grounding passed down through generations.

Representing the third generation of this esteemed lineage, training alongside family and guided by revered masters, the journey has been one of fierce dedication to preserving the purity, depth, and transformative power of the art.

Bridging heritage and empowerment

Ancient principles, answering modern life.

While Kalaripayattu is one of the oldest martial arts in the world, its core principles — focus, breath control, resilience, and unshakeable inner power (Shakti) — are deeply relevant to modern life.

Kalariwoman was born out of a vision to create a dedicated space for women to reclaim this ancient wisdom. For too long, martial arts spaces have felt intimidating or inaccessible. Kalariwoman changes that narrative by offering a nurturing yet rigorous environment where women of all ages can step onto the kalari thara.

Whether it is mastering foundational animal postures (vadivus), building core body mechanics through steps (chuvadus), or engaging with traditional weaponry, every class is designed to help women reconnect with their bodies, build authentic physical strength, and cultivate a quiet, unshakeable mental clarity.

The instructor

Amrutha E.T.

Third generation of the Hindustan Kalari Sangham lineage, and granddaughter of Veerasree Sami Gurukkal. Amrutha grew up on the red earth of the kalari, and now teaches the art she was raised inside.

A decade of offline instruction, and years of guiding students online, sit behind every class. The curriculum is traditional and the standard is not lowered; what changes is the welcome.

This very floor is where I took my first steps, and over the years, it has become a part of who I am.

The journey continues

The mission remains clear.

To honour the gurus who came before us, to protect the sacred integrity of Northern Kalaripayattu, and to ignite the inner strength of every woman who steps onto the path.
